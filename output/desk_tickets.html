What would be simpler as one agent with tools?

Keep the human gate; reduce the handoffs where work is straightforward

Ticket 102 is the clearest single-agent case. One operations agent could read the open board, call `get_rent_notice_context(102)`, call `get_accounting_context(102)`, compare the due date to the shop date and the $2,560 balance after ticket 101, then prepare one approval request. In the actual run, Facilities did the lease read and Boss asked for payment without the planned Accounting delegation. One agent with both read tools would remove a handoff and make the cash check explicit in the same explanation.

Ticket 103 also shows the token cost of splitting a small review too far: Inventory, Accounting, a second Inventory pass, and Customer Service all read the same bulk context. A single analyst could fetch it once, report stock and the margin calculation together, then draft a response. Ticket 101 has a stronger reason to split because stock/vendor constraints, cash, and customer wording are distinct checks, but a single agent could still make the reads once and return one compact report. I would keep payment execution outside either design: the `/approvals` route and a human approval are the right separate control.

Three new problems this toolset could handle

01 / PRODUCT TRIAGE

Another student order is out of stock

For a new order ticket with a recorded SKU, size, and quantity, Inventory could use `get_customer_order_context` and `get_restock_context` to compare stock, vendor categories, lead days, and open invoices. Accounting could check a linked bill and cash with `get_accounting_context`; Customer Service could prepare a truthful status draft. As ticket 101 showed, this can identify a blocked supplier and prepare an affordable invoice request. It can resolve the investigation, but it must not promise exact SKU fit or delivery unless the records support it.

02 / RENT FOLLOW-UP

A lease payment is approaching or late

For another rent notice linked to a lease, Facilities could read the amount and due date with `get_rent_notice_context`; Accounting could compare the date to `desk.date_today`, check `cash_accounts` and `payments`, and submit a human approval request. Ticket 102 is the concrete example: the team distinguished September 2 from the August 31 shop date, and the payment route recorded Deep's approval without overdrawing checking.

03 / PRICE REVIEW

A bulk buyer gives a specific target price

If another club names the requested discount on a product represented in `pricing` and `inventory`, Accounting could use `get_bulk_discount_context` to calculate the resulting margin while Inventory checks the requested quantity. Boss could route a clear recommendation and Customer Service could draft the response. Ticket 103 shows the useful boundary: the current tools can explain cost, list price, and shortage, but a human still has to decide the discount because there is no discount-approval/write tool.

Three problems this toolset cannot finish

01 / BUYING AND RECEIVING

Get the missing 12 hoodies into the shop

Ticket 103 had only 8 of 20 on hand. The agents did not obtain a vendor quote, verify exact product fit, place an order, or receive stock. The purchase approval route can record a human-approved quoted order, but it does not contact a vendor or update inventory on receipt. I would add quote records, approved order placement, and a receiving tool that posts the SKU, size, quantity, and date. Inventory and Accounting would handle the checks; a human would still authorize spend and confirm receipt.

02 / CUSTOMER COMMUNICATION

Send an update and handle the reply

For both the Bulldog tee and hoodie request, Customer Service could draft but not send. The current schema has no customer contact preferences, message status, or reply history, and no MCP send tool. A real customer update workflow would need consent/contact records, a delivery and reply log, and a human-reviewed send action. Customer Service could prepare it; authenticated staff should approve and send it.

03 / SHOP MAINTENANCE

Handle a leak at the Chapel Street shop

Facilities can read lease facts for ticket 102, but it has no maintenance, utility, asset, contractor, or work-order records. A leak or power outage needs tools to log and prioritize a repair, track contractor estimates, schedule approved work, and record completion. I would add maintenance-request, asset, contractor-quote, and work-order tables/tools, with Facilities coordinating and Accounting checking an approved budget. The team should not dispatch a real contractor without human authorization.

My takeaway: The team was strongest when a ticket had concrete facts and a clear boundary: the dates and rent on 102, or the recorded invoice and cash on 101. It was less complete when a task crossed from database analysis into the outside world. The Cash tab makes that boundary visible too: $3,400 at reset, $840 for invoice 501, $2,400 for rent, and $160 left; ticket 103 did not justify another outflow from the records we had.
